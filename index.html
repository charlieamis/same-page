How to play

You and a base model are a team. Like the classic TV newlywed game, you score points every time one of you correctly predicts what the other said.

  1. Round 1 · You predict the model. The model answers a question “offstage” and its answer is sealed. You guess what it wrote. 3 questions, 5 points each.
  2. Round 2 · The model predicts you. The model seals its guess first, then you give your own answer. 3 questions, 10 points each.
  3. Bonus question · 25 points. One last, bigger question. The model predicts you.

Answers are a few words. Small differences don't matter (“a dog” = “Dogs”). If the host's matcher is too strict, you can make the host's call and count it, just as the show's host would. A perfect game is 70 points.

    What this does (and does not) mean
    • The model isn't a person and has no favorite season. A base model continues text. The game shows it a log of a prediction game between “Player A” (you) and “Player B”, and the model writes the next line.
    • Round 1: the model's “answer” is what it writes on Player B's line. We sample it 10 times at temperature 1 and use the first usable sample; the others show what it might have said.
    • Round 2: the model's “guess” is what it writes on your line, using everything you've written earlier in the log. It's a prediction of you, and it can only use what's on the page.
    • The percentages are the model's own probability for your exact words (from logprobs), ending with a line break so “pizza” doesn't get credit for “pizza with olives”.
    • One sample is luck as well as skill. A low-probability answer can still come up, which is part of the fun, and part of what base models are like.
